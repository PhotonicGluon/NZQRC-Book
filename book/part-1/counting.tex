\Chapter{Counting and Cardinality}
\Lettrine[loversize=0.1,nindent=2pt]{Ironically}, it is often the case that rigorously defining something intuitive turns out to be exceedingly tricky. Such is the case of counting. Counting the number of things in a collection seems obvious, but creating a watertight protocol for counting is more challenging than it appears at first glance.

\section{The Problem With `Intuitive' Counting}
One would be hard-pressed to disagree that counting is such an obvious and trivial task that it is simply not worth dedicating an entire chapter to it. The very idea of saying how many things exist is, to us, deeply ingrained in our biology. Why do we need to define counting?

The core issue with counting is ensuring everyone agrees on how many items are there. Surely this task is trivial! Simply tally how many things there are! How hard can it be to make everyone agree on the number of items?

In the spirit of Socratic dialogue, let us examine two characters attempting to agree on how many shapes were drawn on a field.

\begin{dialogue}{Archilles}{Tortoise}[1.5em][1.5em]
    \item \textit{Achilles, the mightiest of Greek warriors, waits on a barren field for his friend. While waiting, he draws several shapes in the dirt using his mighty sword.}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=5cm]{images/part-1/counting/five-count_items-only.png}
    \end{figure}
    \textit{At this moment, his friend the Tortoise, who beat a hare in a race, arrives.}

    \item \personA Ah, Tortoise, my friend! Glad to see that you finally made it!
    \item \personB I'm sorry I took so long. I had to win a race against a particularly smug hare.
    \item \personA I'm glad that you did! Come, join me on this field.

    \item \textit{And so Achilles and the Tortoise sat down. The Tortoise notices the shapes that Achilles drew on the ground.}
    
    \item \personB How curious; why have you drawn these shapes on the ground?
    \item \personA I was waiting for you; while doing so, I contemplated the nature of counting.
    \item \personB Counting? The thing that every sentient being can do?
    \item \personA Indeed. A nagging thought kept creeping up in my mind: \textit{how do we count?}
    \item \personB Hah! Isn't it obvious? Just label the objects on the ground, one by one! For example, there are six shapes here.
    \item \textit{Achilles was shaken out of his revere upon hearing that comment.}
    \item \personA \textit{Six}? My friend, that's absurd! How do you prove that there are six shapes here?
    \item \personB I'm surprised you don't see it. Here, let me label the shapes from one to six to prove them to you.
    
    \item \textit{And so the Tortoise labels the shapes as follows.}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=5cm]{images/part-1/counting/five-count_noninj-nonsurj.png}
    \end{figure}

    \item \personB See? I've provided a labelling of the shapes. There are six shapes here.
    \item \personA What? You can't label a shape more than once! That's --
    \item \personB Oh, my apologies. Then there are three shapes here. Let me label them for you.

    \item \textit{And so the Tortoise relabels the shapes.}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=5cm]{images/part-1/counting/five-count_yesinj-nonsurj.png}
    \end{figure}
    \textit{Achilles is stunned by the Tortoise's solution.}

    \item \personA No, that's not a valid labelling. Every shape needs to have at least one label.
    \item \personB So, something like this? I don't consider that spiky thing after the circle a shape, so I'll label the shapes as such.
    
    \item \textit{The Tortoise labels the shapes again, crossing out the star.}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=5cm]{images/part-1/counting/five-count_no-star.png}
    \end{figure}
    
    \item \personB See? Four shapes.
    \item \personA Alright, I'll play along. Let's suppose, for the sake of argument, that the spiky thing (which is called a \textit{star}, mind you) is a shape. How many shapes are there now? 
    
    \item \textit{The Tortoise thinks for a moment\dots}
    \item \personB Seven.
    \item \textit{\dots and labels the shapes as follows.}
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=5cm]{images/part-1/counting/five-count_noninj-yessurj.png}
    \end{figure}

    \item \personB See? Even the spiky shape (you said it's a \textit{star}?) is labelled! Surely you can't disagree now!
    \item \textit{Achilles, fed up with this discussion, walks away.}
\end{dialogue}
